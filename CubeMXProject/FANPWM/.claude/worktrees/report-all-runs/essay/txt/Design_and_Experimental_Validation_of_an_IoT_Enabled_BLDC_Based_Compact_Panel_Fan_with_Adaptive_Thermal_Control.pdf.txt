Abstract-Thermal management is important on the 
reliability of industrial control panels and electronic enclosures. 
The traditional fixed -speed AC induction motor fans are on 
demand and run continuously which is not necessary, thus 
wastage of power and high levels of acoustic noise. In this paper, 
the design and development of a small BLDC motor -driven 
panel fan, which regulated the speed depending on the 
temperature and could monitor itself using IoT technology, will 
be presented. Electronic speed control is a form of speed control 
of the fans in which the fan speed is automatically varied 
depending on the changing thermal conditions and this lowers 
the power consumption without compromising the efficiency of 
the cooling. The results of the experiments prove high energy 
efficiency, reduced noise, and more flexibility in the functioning 
in comparison to conventional AC fans. The IoT integration 
allows performance data logging, remote monitoring and 
control. 
Keywords—BLDC motor, panel cooling fan, adaptive thermal 
control, PWM speed control, IoT -based monitoring, energy -
efficient cooling 
I. INTRODUCTION 
One of the most important specifications in industrial 
control, electronic enclosures and automated systems is 
thermal management because as the complexity of a device 
increases, the amount of heat produced increases and this heat 
can negatively impact the reliability, performance and service 
life of a system. The conventional axial cooling fans have been 
widely employed because they are simple and cheap but 
increasing demands of compactness and efficiency indicate 
weakness of conventional cooling methods 
The majority of panel cooling is based on constant-speed 
single-phase AC induction motors, which are turned on and 
off neither conditioned by the thermal load nor by any other 
factors. They may be mechanically strong but have a low 
energy efficiency and a high acoustic noise level, which 
causes unreasonable power consumption and lack of 
operational flexibility. 
The Brushless DC (BLDC) motor technology has become 
a viable substitute in cooling purposes as it is more efficient, 
can be electronically controlled in speed, and has a longer 
working life span. BLDC fans can operate in variable speed 
with an electronic controller so that the speed of the fans can 
be controlled according to the thermal needs which makes 
them consume less energy and also produce less noise. 
The new developments in Internet of Things (IoT) 
technologies also make it possible to monitor, remotely 
control, and make records of the parameters of cooling 
systems in real time. Nevertheless, the current literature has 
mostly concentrated on control algorithms and simulations but 
little on practical experimentally proven, miniature-sized, and 
adaptable thermoregulated and IoT-monitored, BLDC based 
panel fans. The paper outlines the design and development of 
a small BLDC motor driven panel fan with adaptive 
temperature based speed control and IoT based for monitoring 
and proves to be more energy efficient, low noise and 
operation flexibility in industrial panel cooling 
II. RESEARCH OBJECTIVES 
The primary objectives of this research are: 
1. To develop and manufacture a compact BLDC 
driven panel fan suitable for cooling industrial 
control panels and electronic enclosures. 
2. To study a comparative evaluation of traditional 
AC- induction-motor fans and BLDC -driven fans 
with respect to energy consumption, acoustic 
performance, and operational characteristics. 
3. To implement temperature -based monitoring for 
real- time evaluation of thermal conditions in the 
cooling environment. 
4. To devise an adaptive speed -control scheme that 
automatically adjusts fan speed in response to 
temperature changes, thereby saving energy while 
maintaining effective cooling. 
5. To incorporate IoT -based wireless connectivity 
enabling remote monitoring, control, and data 
logging of key system parameters. 
6. To simulate the performance of the BLDC motor 
with & without controller in MATLAB Simulink

The techniques of electromagnetic design and motor drive 
have a high impact on the efficiency of BLDC motors. 
Permanent-magnet and axial -flux motors are the focus of 
theoretical studies that mathematically derive the torque, 
magnetic flux density and efficiency [3], [4] Later studies of 
sensor- less and electronically commutated BLDC drives 
underline the importance of electronics and control techniques 
concerning drive efficiency and cooling [5], [6]. Although 
these works do form an effective theoretical background, they 
are limited to motor behaviour as opposed to ensuring the 
integration of the cooling system. 
The studies of thermal management also emphasize the 
significance of airflow nature and fan shape to promote 
successful cooling. The works of computational fluid 
dynamics (CFD) are oriented to the analysis of the axial fan 
performance based on the fluid flow equations, the heat -
transfer equations or airflow  dynamics, airflow pressure and 
cooling capacity equations [7], [8]. Such studies validate that 
the design of fans, swirling behaviour and design patterns 
contribute significantly to the cooling performance. However, 
the majority of CFD-related research is simulation-based, and 
does not involve real life experimentations in the industry. 
Other researchers are focused on general thermal design of 
electronic equipment with the focus on airflow, heat sources, 
and enclosures [9], [10]. These sources emphasize the fact that 
cooling systems are supposed to be responsive to varying 
thermal loads and not be set up to operate at fixed settings so 
that variable fan-speed control can be required to ensure less 
energy is consumed but operating temperatures are kept 
within safe limits. 
The recent advances in the field are such activities as the 
adoption of smart control and connectivity in coolers. Internet 
of Things (IoT) offers real -time monitoring, control, and 
optimisation operations on the basis of data [11]. These types 
of systems have predictive maintenance capabilities, as well 
as energy -aware capabilities, and are consistent with more 
modern ideas on smart infrastructure and energy management 
practices [12]. However, the present condition with the art of 
the literature considers the integration aspect as being largely 
software-driven, with minimisation of hardware, 
electromagnetic optimisation, or testing. The reviewed 
literature reveals that the application of the BLDC motor 
technology, CFD to optimise the airflow  and IoT to monitor 
the performance are a unique strategy of improved cooling 
performance. There is a literature gap in the experimentally 
tested panel-cooling designs optimised to the BLDC motors to 
the adaptive speed control and the performance monitoring in 
the form of IoT. That shortcoming has been discussed in this 
paper. 
IV. METHEDOLOGY 
A. Project Development Approach 
The project used a staged development approach which 
incorporated the evolution of the project with increasingly 
advanced features in five different releases. The incremental 
approach allowed the systematic analysis of all technological 
improvements and the determination of the best design 
parameters. 
• Study Phase: A thorough market research on leading 
manufacturers such as Rexnord, EBM Papst, Hicool, Sunon, 
Delta and ID Ideal to find out the industry standard and 
performance levels. 
• Reverse Engineering: The process of systematic 
disassembly and analysis of a number of chosen fan models to 
file techniques and material choices as well as the key design 
factors. . 
Even though this project is defined by five different 
revisions of the design it is necessary to mention that 
Revisions 2, 3, and 4 were created as separate functional 
prototypes whereas Revisions 0 and 1 have been added just 
with the aim to study and compare them. The combination of 
temperature control, automatic speed control, and  IoT 
capability with data logging into one unified prototype 
requires a lot of firmware integration, multi -layered PCB 
redesign, EMI prevention, and thermal optimization. Thus, it 
is still in the research to physically integrate all features into a 
single model.

V. HARDWARE IMPLEMENATION 
A. Motor Construction 
The BLDC motor assembly comprised: 
• Stator Core: Laminated M19-grade electrical steel. 
• Stator Windings: Enameled copper wire, 0.4 mm 
diameter, three-phase Y-connection. 
• Rotor Assembly: Four-pole NdFeB N42 permanent 
mag- nets. 
• Bearings: 6800ZZ ball bearings for extended service 
life. 
• Housing: Flame-retardant PBT (UL94 V-0) plastic. 
B. Electronic Control System 
The custom PCB integrated: 
• Microcontroller: ATmega328P (Revisions 1–3) or 
ESP32 (Revision 4). 
• Power Stage: Six N-channel MOSFETs in a three-
phase bridge. 
• Gate Drivers: IR2101 bootstrap drivers. 
• Hall Sensors: Three sensors spaced 120◦ electrically. 
• Protection: Overcurrent (5 A), overvoltage (280 V), 
thermal shutdown (125 ◦C). 
• Temperature Sensor: DHT11 (Revisions 2–4). 
• Power Supply: Isolated 12 V / 5 V buck converter. 
 
C. Testing Infrastructure 
Comprehensive testing utilized: 
• Tachometer: Non-contact optical RPM (between 1 
RPM) 
• Airflow anemometer (±0.1 m/s): made of hot wire. 
• An instrument like a Thermal Camera FLIR E6 to map 
the temperature. 
• Sound Level meter: Class 2 meter in compliance with 
the IEC standard 61672. 
• Oscilloscope 100 MHz bandwidth in the analysis of 
the waveforms.

VII. APPLICATIONS 
The developed small smart fan system meets a set of 
requirements: 
• Industrial Control Panels: The main use is related to 
the cooling of programmable logic controllers, 
contactors, variable frequency drives and power 
electronic apparatus in a control panel and 
switchgear. The device is easily fitted into common 
panel widths hence making the installation process 
and performance to be smooth. 
• Renewable Energy Systems: Thermal management is 
attained to solar inverters, wind turbine converters, 
battery management systems, and DC-to-AC power 
conversion platforms which require long service 
lives. The design of the fan system will be suitable 
to the high level of reliability of the renewable 
energy applications. 
• Transportation Systems: It can be applied to electric 
cars when it comes to battery heating, motor control 
unit, on -board chargers, and heating, ventilation, 
and air conditioners. 
VIII. CONCLUSION 
This work illustrates the gradual development of a small 
panel fan, where the traditional technology of AC has 
changed to an intelligent and IoT -enabled device, with 
brushless DC (BLDC) motors and full data logging functions. 
The sequential development model has assisted in assessing 
and maximizing technological enhancement. 
Major achievements have been: 
1. Thermoregulation that allows smart decision-
making. 
2. Independent speed control, automated and lowers 
the average power consumption by 40. 
3. Effective IoT connectivity and command latency of 
less than 500 00 ms. 
4. Complete performance recording of performance. 
5. Electromagnetic design optimization including air 
gap, stack height and skew angle. 
6. Screening by standardized testing processes and 
adherence to standards. 
The design strategy, optimization approaches and 
performance measurement as discussed in this paper are 
valuable to other development projects in the field of 
thermal management and intelligent appliances. The 
modular architecture of the system enables the adoption 
of features at will, depending on the requirements of the 
application. 
Even though Revisions 2, 3 and 4 were individually 
developed and validated separately, combining them into 
one product is a future goal. The application of 
temperature monitoring, automatic control, wireless IoT 
connection, and logs in a single device implies a high 
level of PCB integration. The prototype design carried 
out on the basis of modularity allows conducting a full 
assessment of every detail, which will help to make a 
small smart fan available in the future.
