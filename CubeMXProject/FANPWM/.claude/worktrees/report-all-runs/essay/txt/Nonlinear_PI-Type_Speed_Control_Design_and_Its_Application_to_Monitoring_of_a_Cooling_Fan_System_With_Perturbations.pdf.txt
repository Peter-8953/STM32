Nonlinear PI-Type Speed Control Design and
Its Application to Monitoring of a Cooling Fan
System With Perturbations
Min-Che Tsai and Chao-Chung Peng , Member, IEEE
Abstract—The increasing power density of modern elec-
tronic systems has intensiﬁed the need for efﬁcient ther-
mal management, where cooling fans play a pivotal role.
However, fan performance is frequently compromised by
external disturbances, aging effects, and sensor limitations.
To address these challenges, this study presents a ro-
bust nonlinear proportional-integral (PI)-type speed con-
trol strategy. Unlike conventional PI or sliding mode-based
designs, the proposed architecture integrates a nonlinear
robustifying term within the integral path. This structural
innovation ﬁlters the switching signal through the integra-
tor and yields a continuous control input. Consequently, it
effectively eliminates chattering while maintaining strong
robustness against perturbations. Furthermore, a linear ma-
trix inequality (LMI)-based design framework is established
to handle system uncertainties. This systematic approach
simultaneously guarantees global asymptotic stability and
optimizes the control gain and convergence rate, even in
the presence of bounded nonlinear aerodynamic uncer-
tainties. Beyond robust control, the proposed method in-
tegrates physical modeling and parameter identiﬁcation to
enable sensorless real-time health monitoring via a digital
twin. This facilitates early anomaly diagnosis without addi-
tional hardware sensors. Extensive experimental validation
across various commercial cooling fan types demonstrates
the robust generalizability and superior tracking accuracy
of the proposed method compared to linear benchmarks.
The developed framework supports real-world deployment
in intelligent thermal management, fault-tolerant fan array,
and condition-based maintenance applications.
Index Terms—Cooling fan system, external disturbance,
intelligent thermal management, linear matrix inequality
(LMI), real-time health monitoring, robust control.

I. I NTRODUCTION
T
HE rapid advancement of artiﬁcial intelligence (AI), high-
performance computing chips, data center servers, and
automotive electronics has led to a dramatic increase in the
power density of modern electronic devices [1], [2]. This surge
in heat generation has heightened the need for efﬁcient thermal
management in electronic system design [3], [4], [5]. Effec-
tive thermal management is crucial for ensuring the long-term
reliability, operational safety, and performance consistency of
electronic components [6]. In high power density environments
with ﬂuctuating thermal loads, fan-based active cooling meth-
ods signiﬁcantly improve thermal management by providing
more efﬁcient and ﬂexible heat dissipation control [7], [8].
Cooling fans are essential in most active cooling systems to
facilitate heat exchange. These fan-assisted cooling methods are
widely applied across various domains, including server rack
thermal management in data centers[9], thermal control in elec-
tric vehicle battery management systems [10], communication
cabinets [11], and industrial automation systems [12].
As fans have become critical components in system op-
erations, any failure or malfunction can lead to overheating
of electronic products, potentially reducing their lifespan, de-
grading system performance, and even causing catastrophic
consequences. This can result in costly repairs and signiﬁ-
cant downtime. Therefore, the development of effective real-
time health monitoring, troubleshooting techniques, and robust
speed control strategies is essen tial for ensuring the reliability
and stability of fan operation [13], [14].
In recent years, numerous studies have investigated health
monitoring and fault diagnosis for cooling fans, aiming to
enhance system reliab ility through intelligent and preventive
techniques. Wang et al.[15] detected potential fan malfunctions
in transformers by analyzing top-oil temperature trends without
additional sensors. Nematirad et al.[16] achieved real-time fault
detection using acoustic signals and learning-based classiﬁers
in air-forced cooling systems. Yu et al. [17] monitored thermal
degradation in traction inverter heatsinks using ANN models
and temperature response analysis. Jin et al. [13] applied Ma-
halanobis distance with mRMR feature selection to identify fan
aging and anomalies under long-term operation. For small fan
motors, Afshar et al. [18] used motor current signals to detect
bearing faults with low computational cost. Kim et al. [19]

proposed a self-powered triboelectric sensor to enable fan status

To facilitate the practical implementation of health monitor-
ing in cooling fans, recent research has focused on several key
technologies, including physical modeling, system identiﬁca-
tion, and state estimation[20], [21], [22]. Advanced methodolo-
gies have been proposed for modeling the dynamics of cooling
fan systems, identifying system parameters, and predicting fan
speed in real time [20], [21]. These approaches enable sen-
sorless online monitoring by constructing virtual models of
the fan system. Moreover, accurate measurement and robust
signal processing are vital for reliable fan monitoring. However,
in practical applications, measurement noise introduces uncer-
tainty that degrades estimation accuracy, increases false alarms,
and complicates diagnostics. To address this, speed estimation
techniques incorporating parameter update mechanisms have
been proposed to correct parameter errors and model uncer-
tainties, thereby improving the robustness and precision of fan
speed estimation [22].
In addition to state monitoring, effective fan speed control
plays a critical role in intelligent thermal management. Given
the widespread deployment of cooling fans in engineering sys-
tems, control strategies are required to be simple, reliable, and
robust. Among them, proportional–integral–derivative (PID)
control remains a mainstream solution due to its ease of im-
plementation and broad applicability [23], [24].
Given that modern high-performance cooling fans share the
fundamental architecture of permanent magnet synchronous
motors (PMSMs), sophisticated strategies developed for PMSM
drives offer potential solutions. Recent studies include model
predictive control utilizing ultralocal models [25] or cascaded
extended Kalman ﬁlters [26] to handle system nonlinearities,
data-driven neural network approaches for prescribed perfor-
mance [27], and event-triggered sliding mode control (SMC)
to enhance disturbance rejection [28]. However, practical de-
ployment in mass-produced fan arrays is constrained by low-
cost microcontrollers that preclude computationally intensive
algorithms. Moreover, the lack of current sensors limits control
bandwidth, and the discontinuous switching of sliding mode
techniques risks violating strict acoustic noise regulations.
To achieve robust stability under these hardware constraints,
linear matrix inequality (LMI ) techniques have been widely
employed in robust controller design and feas ibility analysis.
Recent studies indicate that LMI-based formulations remain a
powerful tool for controller parameter synthesis and stability as-
sessment [29], [30]. In particular, recent works have addressed
proportional-integral (PI) controller gain design under system
uncertainties and external disturbances using LMI approaches,
while satisfying time-domain performance speciﬁcations and
maintaining robustness of the control system [31].
However, in thermal management applications, cooling fans
are subject not only to parametric uncertainties but also to sig-
niﬁcant external disturbances. Typical disturbances include air-
ﬂow ﬂuctuations due to duct pressure changes, supply voltage
variations, and ambient temperature shifts. These factors can
severely degrade fan efﬁciency. In this context, although LMI-
based linear PI control guarantees stability, it may be inadequate
to provide sufﬁcient disturbance rejection without aggressive
gains that could compromise smoothness.
To address this, inspired by [32], [33], and [34], a nonlinear
PI-type speed controller with LMI-based gain optimization is
proposed. Compared to recent work [31], the proposed method
enhances disturbance rejection while preserving practical im-
plementability.
In summary, this study focuses on the robust controller design
for cooling fan systems and integrates the proposed control
strategy into a real-time speed monitoring framework to achieve
accurate trajectory tracking and real-time health monitoring.
The main contributions are as follows.
1) A nonlinear PI-type speed controller is developed for
robust tracking. Unlike conventional integral sliding mode
control (ISMC), where the discontinuous switching gain
acts directly on the control input, the proposed method
places the switching term within the integral path. This
structure ﬁlters the switching signal through the integrator,
producing a continuous control input. This effectively
eliminates chattering while maintaining robust perfor-
mance against external disturbances.
2) An LMI-based design framework is established to handle
system uncertainties. This systematic approach guaran-
tees global asymptotic st ability and optimizes both the
control gain and convergence rate, even in the presence
of bounded nonlinear aerodynamic uncertainties.
3) A closed-loop real-time health monitoring system is de-
veloped based on the proposed robust control framework,
promoting its practical deploy ment in intelligent thermal
management, fault-tolerant fan architectures, and predic-
tive maintenance.
4) Numerical simulations and experimental results validate
the proposed methods effectiveness in disturbance rejec-
tion and superior tracking accuracy compared to recent
studies [31], conﬁrming theoretical and practical con-
sistency. Moreover, extended experimental validation on
various commercial cooling fan types demonstrates the
robust generalizability of the proposed method.

VI. C ONCLUSION
This article proposes a nonlinear PI-type controller for robust
speed tracking of a cooling fan system under model uncertain-
ties and external disturbances. By incorporating a switching
gain, the controller effectively mitigates the effects of both
parametric uncertainties and external perturbations. Theoretical
analysis based on Lyapunov stability and LMIs feasibility con-
ditions conﬁrms that the closed-loop system achieves asymp-
totic stability under the proposed cont rol strategy. To validate
the effectiveness of the method, both numerical simulations
and real-time experiments were conducted on an embedded
cooling fan platform. The results consistently demonstrate that
the proposed controller achieves accurate and robust track-
ing performance, signiﬁcantly outperforming the LMI-based PI
controller. These ﬁndings verify the practical applicability of
the proposed approach and conﬁrm its robustness in uncertain
operating environments. In addition, the proposed control tech-
nique is further integrated with the existing fan digital model
to enable real-time closed-loop health monitoring. Through
intentional disturbance experiments, the system demonstrates
the ability to detect anomalies effectively during the robust
speed tracking process. This extension not only enhances the
diagnostic capability of the fan systembut also highlights its po-
tential for deployment in intelligent thermal management, fault-
tolerant fan arrays, and condition-based maintenance of cooling
units in industrial environments. Given the limitations of the
reduced-order model, future work will investigate observer-
based techniques for drive current estimation. Incorporating the
full second-order model for system identiﬁcation will further
enhance model prediction accuracy and overall control perfor-
mance.

Chao-Chung Peng (Member, IEEE) was born
in Kaohsiung, Taiwan, in 1980. He received the
B.S. and Ph.D. degrees in aeronautics and as-
tronautics from the National Cheng Kung Uni-
versity (NCKU), Tainan, Taiwan, in 2003 and
2009, respectively.
Since 2016, he has been with the Department
of Aeronautics and Astronautics, NCKU, where
he was appointed as an Assistant Professor in
2016, promoted to an Associate Professor in
2020, and to a Full Professor in 2023. In 2024,
he was the Director of the Advanced Intelligent Unmanned Aerial Vehicle
Research and Development Center, NCKU. From 2008 to 2009, he was
a Research Assistant with the Department of Engineering, University
of Leicester, Leicester, U.K. He was a Postdoctoral Fellow with the
Department of Mechanical Engineering, NCKU, from 2010 to 2012.
In 2012, he joined as a Senior Engineer with the Embedded Sys-
tem Development Section, Measurement and Automation Department,
ADLINK Technology Inc., Taipei, Taiwan. From 2014 to 2016, he was
with the Automation and Instrumentation System Development Section
of the Iron & Steel Research and Development Department, China Steel
Corporation (CSC), Kaohsiung, Taiwan. His research interests include
high-performance motion control, unmanned vehicle system design,
advanced ﬂight control, autonomous robotics, intelligent simultaneous
localization and mapping (SLAM), system modeling, and real-time diag-
nostic technologies.
Dr. Peng was elected to the Phi Tau Phi Scholastic Honor Society in
2009. He received the Excellent Y oung Engineering Professor Award
from the Chinese Society of Mechanical Engineers (CSME) in 2019 and
the Rising Star Award from the College of Engineering, NCKU, in 2023.
