Performance Improvement of Brushless DC Motor
Using Improved Super-Twisting Algorithm
Hao Lin∗, Chenhong Shi ∗, Jing Zhang ∗, Zhishu Xu ∗, Hui Zhao ∗
∗Laboratory of Aerospace Servo Actuation and Transmission, Innovation Center for Control Actuators,

Email: linh job@163.com
Abstract—Brushless DC motor is widely used in industrial
production and daily life. In order to meet the increasing
requirements of motor control performance, sliding-mode control
strategy is widely used to improve the dynamic performance and
disturbance rejection ability of motor systems. In this paper, an
improved super-twisting algorithm is designed for a brushless
DC motor control system, which achieves fast convergence of
the rotor position to its reference value under load variations.
Through the comparison with the proportional strategy, the
control performance of the proposed strategy is veriﬁed through
theoretical analysis and simulation under different operation
conditions.
Index Terms—fast dynamic response, active disturbance rejec-
tion control, brushless DC motor, speed control
I. I NTRODUCTION
The traditional brushed DC motor is popular in some
simple applications because of its simple structure, low cost
and high output torque. However, it is difﬁcult to achieve
rapid start and stop, acceleration and deceleration and other
requirements due to its own structural defects caused by
mechanical commutation and brush [1]. The brushless DC
(BLDC) motor overcomes these problems by removing these
components, which improves its lifetime and reduces its com-
plexity. In addition, because of the signiﬁcant features such as
small size, excellent dynamic performance, high reliability and
good torque characteristic, BLDC motors are widely used in
aerospace, industrial robots, CNC machine tools, new energy
vehicles and other ﬁelds [2]–[5].
Normally, the BLDC motor control system is consisted
of three control loops to ensure its stable operation, which
includes a position control loop, a speed control loop and
a current control loop or torque control loop. However, in
actual applications, to reduce the cost and size of DC motors,
the current control loop or both the current control loop and
the speed control loop is not applied in the motor controller
design, which saves the space and cost of the speed or current
sensors [6]–[9]. In this paper, only a position control loop
and a speed control loop are considered for the BLDC motor
control system. In the position control loop, the controller
should be designed to force the rotor position reach its given
values. In the speed control loop, the control objective is to
regulate the rotor speed to its reference value, that is, the
output of the position control loop.
In order to achieve the above two basic control objec-
tives and obtain good system performance, the classical
proportional-integral (PI) control strategy has been widely
used in motor control systems due to its simplicity and easy
implementation [10], [11]. However, the load torque of the
BLDC motor system is not a constant in practical applications,
and it may change with the variations of the rotor position, ro-
tor speed and other system states. Therefore, for the nonlinear
BLDC motor system, the PI control strategy can not achieve
satisfactory performance when the load changes [12]. To meet
the increasing demand of motor control systems including
overshoot, settling time, disturbance rejection performance and
other properties, the sliding-mode control (SMC) strategy is
becoming an attractive alternative. The major advantages of
the SMC include fast dynamic response speed and insensitivity
to disturbances, but it should be noticed that large control
parameters are required to increase the response speed and
reject the negative effect caused by disturbances, which lead
to the chattering problem [13]–[15].
To solve the chattering problem of the SMC strategy
while maintaining its advantages, many methods have been
proposed [16]–[18]. In [19], a robust adaptive super-twisting
algorithm (STA) is combined with a radial basis function
network estimator to strength the robustness and achieve high
precision drive of inductive motors. In [20], an adaptive SMC
law is proposed for a permanent magnet synchronous motor
(PMSM), which suppresses the chattering phenomenon of
system states while retaining the fast convergence of tracking
error. An fuzzy integral SMC strategy is proposed for a
PMSM in [21], which eases the chattering problem caused by
mismatched disturbances. In [22], a STA is proposed for the
direct power control of a BLDC motor, the chattering effect
and discontinuous high-frequency switching are mitigated, and
the ﬂuctuation ripple of the torque is reduced.
In this paper, an improved STA is designed for the BLDC
motor system. In the position control loop, a simple pro-
portional strategy is applied for the rotor position tracking.
In the speed control loop, an improved STA is designed,
which attenuates the chattering phenomenon and achieves fast
convergence of the rotor speed to its reference value under
the variations of the load torque. The control principle of
the proposed strategy is introduced by theoretical analysis,
and the control performance is veriﬁed through simulation

the sliding-mode variable reduces. The functions φ1 and φ2
decrease quickly to suppress the chattering phenomenon, the
improved STA obtains better steady-state performance than the
STA.

V. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, an improved STA strategy is proposed for
the BLDC motor control system to improve the dynamic
performance and disturbance rejection ability. A comparative
study of the proposed strategy and the P-P strategy is pre-
sented through theoretical analysis and simulation veriﬁcation.
Through four groups of simulation including four different
reference curves of rotor position and three types of load
variations, both strategies ensure the stability of the motor
system, the proposed strategy shows better dynamic response
ability and disturbance rejection performance than the P-P
strategy.
